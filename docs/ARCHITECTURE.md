# Architecture

Current accepted contracts, organized by subsystem. For unfinished requirements and measured evidence see [STATUS](STATUS.md); for setup see [DEVELOPMENT](DEVELOPMENT.md). Versions live in Cargo.toml/Cargo.lock and rust-toolchain.toml. Edit the relevant section when a contract changes; do not prepend checkpoint reports.

## Platform and ownership

Windows 10 22H2+ x64, Rust/MSVC. winit owns events; egui renders through the patched egui-directx11 adapter, not eframe/wgpu. FFmpeg supplies media codecs, image/png supply image decoding, and D3D11/DXGI supply presentation.

| Boundary | Owns | Must not expose |
|---|---|---|
| towavue-core | Time, commands, history, geometry, tab and folder contracts | Unsafe code, Windows/FFmpeg types |
| towavue-runtime-windows | Native windows, COM/FFI, FFmpeg, D3D11, WASAPI, Shell, workers/queues/clocks | Native frame/COM/FFmpeg pointers to app or core |
| towavue-app | Event loop, UI state, tab orchestration, command dispatch | Ownership of native decode/render handles |

Unsafe operations stay behind safe runtime interfaces with explicit lifetime, thread, and synchronization invariants. Runtime frame leases own native surfaces; app receives safe metadata/events or RGBA images.

Keep the single-D3D11-device design: hardware decode, video processing, image/UI rendering, and all host windows use the same adapter/device. D3D11VA falls back to software, not CUDA/QSV. Default audio is event-driven WASAPI Shared, not Exclusive.

## Playback, timing, and recovery

- Video and audio use independent demux/decode feeds and bounded queues. Audio output waits must not block video initialization or paused Seek. Do not drop packets or normal audio samples to relieve backpressure.
- Use the selected FFmpeg best streams consistently for playback, preview, waveform, and export. AVIF image display/preview and sequence export explicitly select timed color/auxiliary-alpha tracks: primary still items may have the same IDs and are not animation sources. Static AVIF display selects the primary item and its associated alpha property/reference.
- Media time is integer nanoseconds relative to the input origin. Audio clock is master while audio runs; monotonic anchors handle priming, video-only playback, and drained audio. Drop only eligible late decoded video frames at presentation; retain terminal video during a longer audio tail.
- Seek invalidates generation, clears queues, seeks/flushes, decodes preroll, and primes at the target. Check the selected key packet's PTS and back up a GOP if necessary; TS retains its packet/byte-position path. Do not replace exact frame selection with approximate keyframe display.
- Audio-only workers reconstruct coarse-timestamp scalar-PCM/FLAC/Vorbis/Opus seek anchors by counting prefix packet samples without decoding them. PCM uses packet sizes; FLAC/Vorbis/Opus use FFmpeg's complete-frame header parsers. FLAC decodes from the target packet; Vorbis/Opus retain 250 ms of warm-up. Vorbis accounts for its discarded first packet; Opus decodes initial padding/first output before counting, retaining that decoder's priming state. Reuse sequential timestamp/gap rules; do not infer constant packet sizes. Side data falls back to decoding the remaining prefix except for Vorbis's exact full first-packet priming skip; unavailable parsing or invalid headers also fall back. This still reads the prefix and does not validate skipped compressed payloads. Sample-precision streams retain direct/preroll seeking. Decoder/resampler/parser creation stays on the audio worker. Set the decoder's packet time base before opening so priming removal also adjusts frame timestamps.
- Coarse-timestamp AAC-LC counts declared 960/1024-sample access units only after decoding initial padding and the first output to establish the sample axis. The live decoder must retain the declared profile/frame length, packets must begin with an ordinary audio element, and their durations must agree within container rounding. Other elements, missing/mismatched durations or subsequent side data use decoding for the remaining prefix. Resume decoding 250 ms before the target. Other AAC profiles keep the existing seek path; do not force full-prefix decoding merely to reproduce PNS random state.
- Coarse PCM/FLAC audio-only runs retain at most 512 scalar packet checkpoints in their open demux owner, learned approximately once per second during prefix counting or ordinary one-packet/one-chunk decoding after a sequential/exact start. Do not learn an approximate video-GOP/direct-start audio axis; subsequent side data or delayed/multi-chunk output stops learning for the run. Publish only positions distinct from their preceding/following audio packets; retain no payload or native decoder state. On reuse, explicitly seek the audio stream before a checkpoint, verify position/PTS/size/payload CRC and absent side data, then restore its preceding sample-axis value into the fresh audio pipeline. Cancellation remains checked during the bounded probe; a failed/missing match rewinds through the original prefix path. Source length/mtime mismatch disables checkpoints until a fresh input is opened. Other codecs keep their priming paths; an uncached seek still traverses the prefix, but a first seek can reuse already played positions.
- AAC/MP3/Opus/Vorbis audio feeds request 250 ms of decoder warm-up before clipping at the requested time; rewinding reused inputs includes negative-time priming packets. Video retains its separate GOP selection. Ogg/Vorbis and Ogg/Opus seeks back up a cached page boundary to avoid restoring an EOS page from an interior packet timestamp. These paths may decode additional container packets/pages; bit-exact compressed-audio seeks are not guaranteed beyond the tested prefix-counted paths.
- Frame stepping finds distinct actual PTS before/after the displayed frame through a cancellable software probe, not nominal FPS arithmetic. App queues at most 32 directions and rejects stale tab/session/generation/request results.
- Results are scoped to owner and generation. Cancellation is cooperative at I/O/decode/conversion boundaries, not a promise to interrupt arbitrary FFmpeg or OS calls immediately.
- Wheel/volume shortcuts and mute control a tab-local 0–200% listening level, not edits or export. Preserve the level and last nonzero unmute level across same-tab navigation, activation and live window transfer; discard them when the tab closes. Apply listening volume to the output without re-decoding, dirtying history or changing Undo/Redo, saved gain, waveform geometry or an active export request. Timeline gain edits remain the UI for saved volume changes. Existing edit-layer gain is retained independently.
- Rate applies to playback and export. Rate changes preserve pitch but may re-prime; they are not guaranteed gapless.
- Ordinary rate playback retains its two square-root atempo stages and supplies finite EOF context. Publish at most ceil(received input frames / exact f32 rate) cumulatively; hold excess until more input arrives and discard output beyond that bound only at EOF. Use actual received samples, not duration estimates, and leave 1x playback byte-preserving. Ordinary non-timeline export uses its separate filter chain and the export sample-count contract below.
- Device removal pauses affected sessions and rebuilds all host surfaces on one new device before restoration. Failed recovery retains edits and stopped positions; Retry must not command dead workers. Endpoint recovery preserves the same ownership discipline.
- Fatal playback errors pause the affected active or retained clock and best-effort pause its output without replacing the original error. Late first-frame or audio-drain events must not start a clock unless playback is Playing. Successful graphics restoration does not clear an existing playback failure or turn it into a resumable pause, including at a playback-range boundary.
- HDR/10-bit presentation must follow an explicit validated color path; do not treat unverified passthrough or ignored metadata as HDR support.

## Windows, tabs, and input

- Keep content panels/input fields black and floating windows, menus, tooltips and the palette on shared #0c0c0c; tabs retain their separate state colors. Compact seek track/progress span the full width in idle and active states; only the handle center's travel is inset, with unchanged pointer/value mapping.
- The host shares graphics across windows. Normal launches from the same SID/session/executable forward path-only requests with acknowledgment; failure must not silently create a duplicate host. No live-state transfer between independent old processes.
- Tabs own history, export target/options, image/view/reading state, transport, focus role, and scroll positions. Background audio continues. Hidden video limits decode work and retains a frame without pinning an entire decoder surface pool.
- Playing tabs with a live, undrained audio output show a speaker before the filename, or a mute icon when their listening level is zero. Paused, ended, failed and audio-less tabs omit it. Clicking the icon toggles that tab's listening mute without activating it or changing edits; its separate hit region does not initiate tab dragging. The icon does not represent saved timeline gain or measured signal loudness; accessibility keeps the plain filename, describes audio state separately and names the mute/unmute button. Background state transitions request redraw without continuously repainting static foreground media.
- Moving a tab transfers its live state and shared image data only after the destination is ready; failure leaves the source intact. Filmstrip tear-off opens the original file independently, without copying edits. Hit testing respects actual window occlusion.
- Native DWM caption controls coexist with an input-transparent child render surface. Preserve native hit testing, resizing, system controls, DPI-aware client geometry, monitor work-area placement, grab offsets, and fullscreen restoration.
- Refresh the child caption cutout before presentation as well as resize: native button bounds can settle later without another client-size event. Cache the applied size/cutout so unchanged frames do not rebuild native regions or invalidate the parent; publish the cache only after a successful region update.
- The toolbar owns a fixed 32-point visible height, independent of native button height. Account for the maximized hidden strip once, using the input frame's safe area. Reserve/cut out the actual button bounds from [TITLEBARINFOEX](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winuser/ns-winuser-titlebarinfoex), with DWM/default fallback when unavailable; retain OS-owned button placement and outer-edge area rather than drawing or relocating native controls.
- Menu, palette, grid, keyboard, pointer, and UIA dispatch shared commands/context rules. Text/IME and explicit custom bindings take precedence over implicit aliases. Do not bypass modal or editing-context guards.
- Menus follow input modality across the menu tree: pointer opening/movement releases fixed keyboard highlights; arrows or UIA enter keyboard navigation. The first Down after pointer use selects the first enabled item. Direct logo menus render the same command content as nested menus and remeasure on opening rather than inherit another section's dimensions.
- Gestures have one owner and commit once on release; Escape, focus loss, stale identity, or incompatible state cancels and restores pre-drag state. Preserve event-time pointer/modifier order, including multiple events in one frame.
- Tab reordering keeps tab geometry fixed until release, with an insertion line instead of a floating-tab projection. Edge scrolling moves the whole strip without changing order. The fixed-thin floating scrollbar owns its presses independently of tab dragging; ordinary and Shift wheel scroll the strip.
- App scroll areas use `scroll_style::ScrollAreaStyle` for pill handles without changing their content's widget visuals, IDs or hit geometry. Idle/content-hover states retain the handle without a track background; bar hover/drag may show the track. Keep the tab strip's fixed thin width and image/filmstrip inset hit regions.
- The window host owns native Move/NoDrop cursor feedback during tab drags, including outside the source client. Reorder, merge and detach share Move; invalid destinations use NoDrop. Restore the last normal UI cursor on release/cancellation, and invalidate drag ownership immediately on native cancellation events. No OLE drag loop, custom cursor bitmap or helper window is required.
- Non-active wheel input is processed only when the OS delivers it and the local hit/context permits it; it must not steal focus. Selection and right-drag editing still require focus.
- Native file dialogs and ordinary unsaved confirmations use owned STA work. Unsaved confirmation defaults to Cancel. Stateful export progress, long errors, and editing forms stay in egui.
- Heavy work, metadata/stat calls, Shell calls, dialogs, and export must not block the UI thread. Redraw on input, results, animation, or explicit deadlines, not unconditional idle polling.

## Images, reading, and previews

- Decode static images and animations to safe RGBA frames with delays and total animation plays (0 means infinite). GIF repeats become total plays, including one play when undeclared; APNG/WebP use declared total plays and AVIF uses its validated edit-list repetition, defaulting to one play when absent. GIF reuses the underlying header parser on the same cancellable file, then rewinds for ordinary compositing; no second frame decode. Image-crate decoders apply EXIF orientation; AVIF applies quarter-turn/reflection display matrices after merging paired FFmpeg color/gray output. Animated PNG uses the runtime compositor.
- Static PNG foreground/prefetch converts decoded rows directly into the retained RGBA8 canvas, then applies EXIF orientation. Preserve palette/low-bit expansion, rounded 16-to-8-bit conversion, caller/512 MiB canvas limits, cooperative row/read cancellation and tail validation. Non-interlaced PNG avoids a full raw color buffer; Adam7 still needs one bounded raw buffer. Codec/metadata/orientation allocations are additional working memory, not a whole-process cap. APNG retains its separate compositor.
- FFmpeg-backed image timing uses a positive next-PTS interval, or the decoded source frame duration when no such interval exists (including the last frame and first-frame-only previews). Only unknown timing falls back to 100 ms; viewing retains its 10 ms minimum. Software frame duration is a safe value, not a native-frame reference.
- AVIF shares bounded ISO-BMFF control reading with export. Still alpha uses pitm/iref/iprp associations; sequences use timed track IDs, media time bases/durations and validated auxi links. Independent color/alpha demux cursors avoid buffering an entire unmatched plane sequence. Merge only equal-sized/equal-timed frames, before either preview callback or retained publication. Still image items have no presentation timing: discard synthetic demux timestamps/durations so a single-item plane can pair with a grid; timed tracks retain strict timestamp/duration validation. First-only loading stops after that merged frame; use low-delay decode without forcing one worker. Retained RGBA follows the caller budget, with a 512 MiB individual canvas limit; decoder/scaler buffers and dual demux contexts are additional working memory, not a whole-process cap. Cancellation is checked between native calls and packets, not inside a codec call.
- APNG snapshots the affected region immediately before PREVIOUS blending, publishes the displayed canvas, then restores it. First animation PREVIOUS acts as BACKGROUND. An independent default poster is not an animation frame. OVER uses the image crate's pixel operation.
- APNG expands low-bit/palette colors and strips 16-bit to 8-bit. Retained RGBA has a caller budget; canvas + raw frame + PREVIOUS region have a 512 MiB working limit. Neither is a whole-process memory cap.
- Foreground loading is latest-only. A separate prefetch worker can hand an in-progress decode to foreground rather than decoding twice. Normal prefetch uses up to nine neighbors; reading prefetch covers the next spread within budget.
- Decoded-cache hits borrow shared pixel ownership rather than consume entries. A cancelled request or smaller caller budget must not discard a valid hit needed by a newer request; source-stamp invalidation, bounded eviction and explicit clearing still release cache ownership.
- Shared decoded-image cache is bounded to 10 images/256 MiB; display texture cache to 8/256 MiB. Texture-cache entries hold weak decode identities, so they do not extend original-pixel lifetimes after the decoder cache and live presentations release them. Open tabs may pin additional data. Managed textures retain GPU resources, dimensions and samplers, not CPU pixel shadows. Whole uploads release their source reference after creation; validated partial rectangles use UpdateSubresource on the serialized same-device immediate context. Deferred partial updates fail explicitly. Driver transfer/storage allocations are additional working memory.
- First-frame previews are borrowed during decoding; optional JPEG/BMP fast paths and cached thumbnails reduce initial waiting. Previews preserve original dimensions/orientation and never become edit, copy, or export sources.
- For normal one-image navigation, retain the previous full image until the next is ready and presented; queue at most 256 direction commands. During the handoff, disable editing/export/image or path copy. Direct destinations, failures, and incompatible state cancel queued directions.
- Last-image-tab close clears original-image caches even when audio/video tabs remain. All-window-empty GPU Trim occurs once after an empty presentation and one idle second; suppress/cancel it during new media or other work. Do not recreate/flush the device or trim while any media remains.
- Hidden/minimized native windows neither advance image/reading-page animation textures nor schedule animation-only wakeups. The same applies while resampling or an unresolved image-edit failure prevents display; a failed reading page alone does not freeze other valid pages. Keep decoded frames, remaining plays and absolute deadlines; catch up to elapsed animation time on return without reopening the source. Finite animations stop on their last frame and remove their animation deadline. Skip whole elapsed cycles without exceeding the remaining count; edits, tab retention and window transfer preserve that count. Lack of focus alone does not suspend animation. Audio/video polling and unrelated deadlines are unaffected; this is not full window-occlusion detection.
- Host thumbnail cache: 64 entries/16 MiB RGBA, dimensions up to 240×160; duration-success cache: 64 entries; disk preview cache: 64 MiB. Deduplicate requests and reject stale results. Valid generated pixels remain reusable in memory if optional disk storage fails; memory hits do not retry storage. Persistence can retry after eviction or in a new cache instance. Video previews share bounded 16-frame sheets, with single-frame fallback while preparing.
- Within the same hovered video tab/path, an installed sheet serves position changes by updating UV coordinates before painting, without requesting or uploading it again. Background-video hover reads the matching retained playback clock and maps edited time back to source time; refresh once per second only while that hovered video is playing, not for paused or unhovered tabs. A different sheet or owner replaces the request; hover exit cancels work and releases its texture. Failed uploads and single-frame fallbacks never supply a reusable sheet layout.
- Loaded image-tab hover borrows the matching active image texture or a retained image from the current graphics epoch, including materialized edits and the presented animation frame. Do not start another thumbnail job, upload pixels or retain a second long-lived texture for this path. Background animations do not advance just for hover. Missing/mismatched presentations and stale graphics epochs retain the shared file-thumbnail fallback; hover/drop-overlay ownership is unchanged.
- Cold JPEG filmstrip generation persists the reduced preview through the same coalesced PNG cache path as other generated thumbnails. Plain BMP sampling remains memory-only when no disk entry exists; honor existing disk entries. Foreground speculative previews and memory hits do not acquire new persistence work. Stored source-dimension metadata accepts the normal 512 MiB original-image budget, without increasing thumbnail pixel limits or the 128 MiB static-decode budget.
- Preview request replacement keeps a still-wanted in-flight lookup/generation and unconsumed results for the same path/media-kind key, publishing under the latest request generation. Reorder pending work and check the shared cache before generating misses, including entries warmed by other consumers since the previous request. Removing a key, clearing, or closing cancels its work; readding it cannot revive a cancelled result. The single worker and 64-unique-key bound remain; do not block UI on native cancellation.
- Welcome prepares supported recent-file thumbnails before their cards scroll into view: visible cards first, then the rest of the list, without duplicates and within the existing 64-request limit. Reuse the filmstrip worker/cache and retain prepared textures while in that working set; disabled recent-file UI cancels its requests and releases those textures.
- Closed filmstrip prepares up to 64 Shell-ordered neighbors, nearest first, while paused/ended and after this window's image loader/prefetch jobs finish. Loading, editing, queued navigation, export, pending folder order and command/modal/drop overlays defer speculation; a foreground image request cancels it immediately. Image work-completion notifications are separate from pixel publication, with cancelled prefetch leases counted until they return. Reuse existing workers, not a polling timer or system-wide idle scheduler. Resolve filmstrip's initial sizing pass before presenting its first frame so prepared textures are visible on opening.
- Non-interlaced static PNG thumbnails sample decoded rows into the bounded target instead of retaining full-resolution RGBA; orientation and 16-to-8-bit rounding match the existing nearest-resize path. Keep source-size limits, row/read cancellation and tail validation. APNG and interlaced PNG do not use this row-sampling path. This thumbnail-only path must not add a full decode ahead of foreground image loading; codec buffers remain additional working memory.
- GIF, APNG and animated WebP thumbnails reuse the original decoder's first composited frame when static prefetch reports an animation, then apply the existing bounded nearest resize/cache PNG path. Preserve first-frame alpha, APNG poster exclusion, source dimensions and cancellation/budgets. Do not decode the whole animation or start an external helper on this supported path; retain the fallback when native thumbnail generation is unavailable. AVIF's existing first-frame path is unchanged.
- Image zoom is cursor-anchored and reflects raw wheel events immediately; actual size uses physical pixels. Pan only overflowing axes. Selection has corner/edge resize, Shift ratio retention, in-bounds movement, and outside-click cancellation. Clicking inside zooms to the selection and clears it without editing pixels; the command shares this behavior, without a dedicated status-bar button. The removed crop-preview mode must not return.
- Reading is display-only: 2–10 joined pages, independent first-spread count, axis and order reversal, non-overlapping navigation. Dirty images cannot enter reading; editing/Undo/Redo is disabled there.
- Clipboard copies edited full-resolution pixels or the selection, the current animation frame, or the current source page in reading. Text focus keeps normal text-copy behavior.

## Editing and export

- Source files remain unchanged. Each tab has ordered non-destructive operations and Undo/Redo. Preview and export share operation geometry, but interpolation/compression can prevent byte identity.
- Dirty comparison uses the saved snapshot, not operation count alone: normalize effective settings, compare exact duration-aware timeline intervals, and compare all image pixels when needed. Crop/flip/quarter-turn comparisons use validated signed-stride views of the original RGBA, with bounded cancellation checks and no rendered copy; other raster edits retain the FFmpeg-rendered path. Reuse materialized display pixels; compare on the existing worker, reject stale evidence, and remain dirty while pending. Image tabs retain only accepted comparison results with their current/saved operation snapshots across activation and window transfer, without another pixel buffer. Cancelled/incomplete comparisons restart; changed snapshots invalidate reuse. Undo/Redo history is retained.
- Raster operations apply in order. Crop uses integer pixels (image 1px, video even pixels); the current H.264 path requires at least 16×16 video. Respect SAR/orientation and validate source geometry before applying stored operations.
- Resize exposes Nearest, Bilinear, Bicubic, and Lanczos. Arbitrary rotation uses 0.1-degree geometry and a bounded containing canvas; image alpha interpolation is premultiplied. Video GPU raster stays on the shared device with bounded intermediates, while export is separately encoded. Do not promise GPU/export byte identity or full HDR fidelity.
- Materialized image edits preflight every frame's output geometry and the total 512 MiB retained-output budget before allocating output pixels or building filters. No partial edited animation is published. The resize dialog uses the loaded image's frame count to disable oversized results and explain the animation budget; video retains its per-canvas limit. Source pixels, native filter buffers and GPU textures are additional memory, not part of this retained-output cap.
- Image materialization, rendered dirty comparison and streamed WebP export reuse one worker-owned FFmpeg spatial-filter graph per operation list and contiguous input-size run. An empty operation list instead copies validated RGBA in cancellable 64 KiB chunks, preserving hidden RGB/alpha and delays without a native graph. Current/saved comparison sides have independent graphs; borrowed orthogonal comparisons create none. Rebuild on size changes, emit one output per input with monotonic internal PTS, and retain original frame delays. Graphs do not survive their job or create a global image cache.
- Single-source EditTimeline uses half-open intervals and exact integer mapping for Delete/Keep, gain, stretch, playback, waveform placement, and export. Empty timelines are Undoable but cannot be exported. Waveform rearrangement is an overview, not exact post-tempo PCM.
- Timeline audio borrows each segment's PCM slice from the current decoded chunk, using the same rounded sample origin and half-open bounds as owned decoder clipping. Keep the original chunk intact across adjacent edits; empty intersections allocate no intermediate PCM. Tempo copies accepted input before returning, so borrowed bytes never outlive the callback. For independently decodable PCM/FLAC, count complete packets in deleted gaps without decoding them; retain sequential demux, the same decoder/sample axis, and normal boundary-packet clipping. Counting requires a trusted sequential/coarse sample axis or an exact packet timestamp after a direct seek; missing direct-seek timestamps cannot initiate it. Checkpoint learning remains coarse-only. Reuse prefix-counting rules; side data or delayed/multiple decoded chunks disables gap counting for the run. Other codecs retain continuous decoding. This is not demux skipping, a changed tempo boundary, or an end-to-end zero-copy path.
- Timeline audio playback/export give each atempo stage two analysis windows of finite zero-valued EOF context before trimming to the planned output sample boundary. This releases sub-window input instead of replacing a short edited span with silence. The playback sink can finish before the source interval; subsequent input and repeated finish calls must not add output. Do not use an infinite padding source inside the in-process drain. This is not seamless reconstruction of arbitrary cut points or a guarantee of non-silent slow-motion tails; ordinary non-timeline rate processing remains separate.
- Video visual editing is enabled only while the timeline is visible. Audio timeline remains visible even in fullscreen. Starting playback inside a half-open time selection activates its temporary playback range; starting outside ignores it. Explicit selected playback starts at its beginning. The active range stops at its end with repeat off, or loops within the same file with video repeat / either enabled audio repeat mode. Selection playback never advances the audio folder queue. Escape, selection changes and seeking outside release the range; timeline edits clear it.
- Video visual-selection interior clicks and Zoom to selection share the image fit-and-clear behavior, gated by the visible timeline. Fit uses the displayed pixel aspect and edited orientation; update the visible rectangle/UV in the input frame without changing playback, time selection or edit history. Right-drag starting inside moves only the selection on its 2px grid, preserving its pixel extent and clamping to the edited frame; AllScroll feedback and cancellation restoration share the image path. An outside-started right-drag remains pan even when it crosses the selection.
- Video repeat is a tab-local viewing preference, retained across activation, live window transfer and same-tab video navigation. It loops the current playback range without an audio folder queue or video auto-next. Its button/customizable Ctrl+Alt+R binding leaves Ctrl+R resize unchanged. Paused EOF previews do not auto-start; empty timelines do not loop. Audio's existing Off/All/One and shuffle behavior remains unchanged outside selected playback.
- The CTI triangle owns Seek dragging with a horizontal-resize cursor; its line does not. Its triangle and one-physical-pixel stem share one snapped axis and mesh. Timeline contents reach the rounded background's edges without internal padding; external panel margins remain.
- Time selection supports endpoint resize, gain dragging, and Alt stretch. A selection press seeks immediately; dragging previews the selection beginning without repeatedly restarting decode, and a reverse drag seeks its earlier beginning on release. Cancellation preserves the prior selection/history but does not undo the press seek. Gain-line presses wait for the gesture direction before seeking. CTI/selection paint survives discarded passes without replaying input actions. Selection uses white 20% difference fill and two 1px dotted boundaries; image selection retains its separate inversion outline.
- Exports run cancellably into owned staging files. Verify source identity, requested metadata, output validity, and cancellation before publication; failed/cancelled work must preserve an existing target. History and saved baseline change only for the matching successful request.
- Audio-only export, peak normalization, and mono/stereo options are explicit per-source output settings. Metadata uses Keep/Set/Remove; UI reads existing values asynchronously and rejects stale snapshots.
- Ordinary non-timeline audio rate export counts the selected stream's trimmed decoded samples in a cancellable null-muxer pass; never infer its length from container duration or another stream. Supply finite context per atempo stage, then pad/trim to ceil(actual input frames / exact f32 rate). Normalization analyzes this bounded output after channel conversion. Reuse source-stamp checks and staged publication; do not retain a whole-audio PCM buffer or intermediate file. This adds one decode/read pass only for rate-changed ordinary audio/video exports with audio, and makes normalization a three-pass operation. Show indeterminate analysis progress for this path; encoding remains an estimate. Codec framing may still affect the final compressed container's decoded duration.
- PNG/APNG retains ten supported text fields; JPEG and WebP retain nine supported XMP fields, including JPEG↔static WebP conversion. Preserve supported language/author structures and existing noncanonical date/track values under Keep, even without explicit metadata options; Set validates typed values. Use the existing bounded XMP parser/writers and validate the source before encoding and staged values before publication. Animated WebP→JPEG is rejected before encoding, including one-frame animations. Raster conversion retains its normal encoding behavior; JPEG cannot retain alpha. Unknown-XMP interpretation, EXIF/IPTC/COM/ICC synchronization and other cross-format metadata transfer are not implied.
- PNG metadata scanning validates CRC, bounded text (128 chunks / 1 MiB stored or expanded), and animation controls. Supported APNG export retains 1–65536 frames, exact delay fractions and loop count, including separate posters.
- PNG/APNG→PNG/APNG with an empty image-operation list rewrites supported text directly from source chunks, without re-encoding or reducing bit depth. Preserve IDAT/fdAT, palettes/transparency, ICC/EXIF/unknown chunks/text, interlace and raw APNG region/disposal/blend/poster controls. Validate every raw frame and separate poster by consuming decoder rows (including Adam7 passes) before rewriting; no compositing, RGBA conversion, full canvas or sequence-sized pixel buffer is needed. Keep codec/512 MiB raw-frame bounds, row/read cancellation, CRC/text/control/readback and source-identity/publication guards. Other metadata is copied, not interpreted or synchronized; Remove is not a privacy scrub. Raster edits/conversion retain their existing color/metadata limitations.
- JPEG→JPEG with an empty image-operation list rewrites supported XMP directly from the source, preserving every non-XMP byte (compressed samples, EXIF orientation, ICC/IPTC/COM and other markers). Strict cancellable FFmpeg decode-to-null validation precedes the bounded marker splice; source identity, XMP readback and staged publication guards still apply. Actual edits and cross-format output retain their encoding paths. Unselected XMP follows the preservation rule below; XMP Remove does not scrub copies in other metadata.
- WebP→WebP with an empty image-operation list similarly rewrites supported XMP without re-encoding. Preserve compressed static/animated payloads, frame regions/blend/disposal/loops, ICC/EXIF/unknown non-XMP chunks and VP8X extension bytes; only RIFF size, XMP chunks and the VP8X XMP flag/header creation change. Validate every frame with the display decoder, reusing one output buffer with a 512 MiB canvas bound, before the bounded container rewrite and source-identity/readback/publication checks. No whole-process memory cap or performance guarantee; per-frame decode is not interruptible. Actual edits and conversion retain their encoding paths. Unselected XMP follows the preservation rule below; Remove is not a privacy scrub.
- JPEG/WebP same-format saves without image operations retain the validated source XMP packet byte-for-byte under all-Keep. Set/Remove rewrites only the selected top-level properties (expanded namespace names, including attribute forms), retaining other XML events, mixed opaque content, namespace scopes and packet wrappers. New properties declare their own namespaces and reuse a Description without inherited xml:lang/base/space context, adding one only when needed. Repeated updates must not accumulate wrappers; identical Set values retain the packet, while Remove also deletes empty RDF lists. Readback must match the complete prepared packet and requested supported values. Keep the existing single UTF-8 XML packet, 65502-byte, 4096-element/32-level, 128-value bounds, DTD rejection and supported-property shape restrictions; JPEG Extended XMP remains unsupported. Removing all editable fields may leave opaque XMP and its container flag. Raster/format changes use the selective rule below.
- JPEG/WebP raster edits (including animated WebP) and JPEG↔static WebP conversion retain the nine editable text fields plus standard [XMP Rights Management](https://developer.adobe.com/xmp/docs/xmp-namespaces/xmp-rights/) Owner, UsageTerms, WebStatement and Marked properties. Preserve their XML subtrees/attributes and namespace/language context without interpreting or validating licensing claims. Omit other top-level properties, including stale geometry/orientation, asset identifiers and potentially original-resource-specific Certificate references. ## Shell ordering and asynchronous services

Use live Explorer view order first, preferring a foreground matching window; otherwise resolve persisted Shell view/default template through IExplorerBrowser with EBO_NOPERSISTVIEWSTATE. Read public IFolderView2 sort metadata and enumeration; never parse registry Bags or substitute name sorting silently.

Initialize the Shell worker's STA only for a current folder request; retain it across requests and balance it on the owning thread, including unwind. Close/invalidation checks stop stale work between native phases, while waiting for a hidden view, and between enumerated items. Cancellation does not trigger filename-order fallback. The request loop remains message-aware when idle; provider drop signals closure without joining a possibly blocked Shell extension on the UI thread. These checks do not interrupt an in-flight native call or prove process-wide shutdown safety.

A shared immutable FolderSnapshot feeds filmstrip, playlist, and navigation. Filter supported media after Shell enumeration. Only on Shell failure use Windows natural-name fallback and label it. Empty-folder Open must not replace current media/history/navigation.

Shell work stays on a dedicated STA with message-aware waiting (MsgWaitForMultipleObjectsEx), not a condition variable that starves COM windows. Use generation/path checks, latest-request mailboxes, and debounced directory notifications. Refresh order on media load and filmstrip opening; reconcile by item identity/path, not old index.

## UI conventions

Keep a media-first grayscale UI: Figtree, proportional Japanese fallback, tabular numeric glyphs, Codicon, #2C2C2C hover backgrounds, centered shadow offset, and stable left-aligned status values. Play/pause use solid vector counterparts to the Codicon outlines, retaining shared button interaction and focus roles. Tabs reserve 3 logical px top/bottom and the native 1 physical px boundary.

Media notices go in the left status area; image information stays right. Fullscreen uses the bottom bar. Save progress occupies the toolbar boundary, not ordinary image-navigation loading. Volume uses a 1.2s thin HUD without replacing the path notice.

Visual selection reports pixel position and size from the edited/displayed geometry, including retained image navigation handoffs. Video metrics appear only while its timeline is visible and use the even-pixel crop grid, not SAR-expanded display width. While dragging, metrics take priority; after release they follow the path, with existing notices and focused-edge descriptions retaining their priority. An image selection reveals fullscreen status without taking focus or interrupting scrollbar/other gestures. Windowed selection changes schedule a repaint because status is laid out before media input.

Image scrollbar tracks and hit regions are inset without shrinking the displayed image viewport or its pan range. Fullscreen controls yield while the pointer is over the image-bar strip, so they do not cover its drag targets.

Filmstrip shows only the highlighted filename, centered above its thumbnail and wrapped to at most two rows. Primary clicks dismiss it, including the current item; navigating elsewhere retains the unsaved guard. Middle-click registers an unloaded background tab while keeping filmstrip, its scroll/focus, and the current media/edits intact. Its inset scrollbar leaves non-draggable gutters that still accept panel wheel input. Filmstrip and the recent-file grid reveal newly focused cards during keyboard navigation; unchanged focus must not undo manual scrolling. Retain filmstrip's bounded virtualization and the recent list's shared preview preparation. Filmstrip leaves tab reordering/detach gestures available. Closing it restores a pre-existing focus only for the same media generation; otherwise clear its card focus without selecting a tab, Welcome, or fullscreen control.

Media previews are immediate, noninteractive and clipped to valid hover ownership; help tooltips keep their delay and close on clipped/covered/changed targets. The logo's directional menus and normal click/keyboard entry share context/dispatch. Exact widget values live with UI code and regression tests.
